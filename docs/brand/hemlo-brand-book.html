Elegantly solved.

Hemlo is an Iraq-first software company that builds small, focused products for the everyday work of businesses. We study the work first. Then we build the simplest thing that fits it.

Positioning statement

For Iraqi businesses whose daily work runs on WhatsApp, notebooks and spreadsheets, Hemlo builds focused software products, each designed around one job we have studied inside real workplaces. Unlike general ERPs and imported tools, a Hemlo product does its one job completely, fits how Iraqi businesses actually operate, and has to prove its value in daily use.

Manifesto

Most work in Iraq already has a system. It's a notebook, a WhatsApp group, a stamp and a person who remembers everything.

We don't pretend that system is broken. We study it, find the parts that cost people time or money, and build software only for those parts.

Small products. Real problems. Plain words. Software that works when the signal drops and the day gets busy.

If it doesn't earn its place, we don't ship it.

Positioning

Practical claims only. Everything here should be defensible in a conversation with a sceptical shop owner.

Purpose
Turn the repeated, error-prone parts of everyday work into software simple enough to use every day.

Each Hemlo product does one job completely, and does it the way your business actually works.
Values
Understand first (no code before we've seen the work) · One job, done completely · Plain words · Built for real conditions (weak signal, cash, Arabic, paper) · Earn trust slowly (no promises we can't keep)
Differentiation
Not a generic ERP with 40 modules and not a translated foreign tool. Hemlo is a portfolio of narrow products, each built from field research in Iraqi workplaces, sharing one account, one design language and one support standard.
Target audience
Owners and operators of Iraqi small and medium businesses, plus the accountants, technicians and staff who do the daily work.

Fewer lost records, fewer phone calls asking for status, faster reports and a clear view of money and work.
Emotional benefit

Verifiable from day one: every product starts with published field research, has a narrow scope, and handles Iraqi specifics (IQD, landmark addresses, Arabic and Kurdish, WhatsApp delivery, offline use). Customer proof is added only as it is earned.

06

Brand Principles

Rules for product and design decisions. When two options compete, these break the tie.

1. Understand before building

No feature ships without a real example from a real workplace behind it.

2. One job, done completely

A narrow product that finishes the job beats a wide one that half-does ten.

3. Design for real conditions

Weak signal, a cracked phone, 48°C, cash, paper, Arabic. The hardest condition sets the standard.

4. Localise the details, not the decoration

IQD formatting, landmark addresses and WhatsApp delivery show you're Iraqi. Palm trees and lions don't.

5. Plain words, exact numbers

If a sentence needs a second read, rewrite it.

6. Quiet confidence

One strong idea per screen, one accent per page, no effects that exist only to impress.

07

Brand Story

Walk into almost any business in Iraq and you'll find a system that works better than outsiders expect. Orders arrive on WhatsApp. Debts live in a notebook by the till. A technician's day is planned in a phone call. Receipts are stamped, photographed and forwarded. Someone always remembers where everything is.

That system holds together until it doesn't: a payment nobody recorded, a customer who calls five times for an update, a report assembled by hand at midnight, a shipment held because one document was wrong. The cost is small each time and large over a year.

Hemlo started from a simple decision: don't build software for Iraq from a distance. Sit in the shop, read the notebook, follow the paperwork, then build only the part that genuinely needs software, and make that part simple enough to use on a busy day with a weak signal.

We're early. We don't claim to have transformed anyone's business. What we promise is a method: every Hemlo product starts with real work we've studied, does one job properly, and stays only if it keeps earning its place in people's days.

Naming principles

  • Name what the customer manages, not the industry. "Hemlo Import" reads like an importing company; Hemlo Shipments doesn't.
  • One word, in plain English, that customers already say.
  • Every product has a fixed Arabic descriptor, chosen for meaning rather than translated.
  • No invented product words, no "Pro/Plus/AI", no numbers in names.
  • Plans and editions are named by size (Starter, Team, Business), never by metals or gems.

Hierarchy and the exception rule

  • Level 1: Hemlo (company, account, billing, support)
  • Level 2: Hemlo + descriptor (products)
  • Level 3: plain feature names inside products ("Deposit schedule", "Visit report"), never branded
  • A product may earn an independent name only if it later serves a different audience that doesn't benefit from the parent, and it keeps the endorsement "by Hemlo".
  • The branches are Hemlo Tools (free), Hemlo Studio (custom work), Hemlo Systems (ready-made systems, one edition per trade, starting with repair workshops) and Hemlo School.

Voice & Tone

A capable colleague who has visited your business, explaining something clearly on a busy day.

Voice principles

  • Specific over impressive. "Send a visit report in one tap", not "streamline reporting".
  • Short sentences, active verbs. One idea per sentence.
  • Customer's words. "Debt", "receipt", "technician", never "AR ledger" or "workforce entity".
  • Numbers exact. 1,250,000 IQD, not "over a million".
  • Technical depth on request. Plain for owners; precise and open for developers.
  • Punctuation: full stops, commas, colons for lists. No exclamation marks in product UI. No ALL CAPS sentences.

Arabic and English tone

  • English: calm, direct, lightly warm. Sentence case everywhere.
  • Arabic product UI: clear Modern Standard Arabic, short, no flowery openings ("نود أن نحيطكم علماً…" is banned).
  • Arabic marketing and social: MSA with Iraqi warmth where it helps (الشغل، هسه sparingly). Never full dialect in written UI.
  • To owners: money, time, control. To staff: less typing, fewer mistakes. To developers: architecture, trade-offs, what didn't work.
  • About products: describe the job, show the screen, state the limit. "Hemlo Shipments doesn't file your customs declaration. Your broker still does."

Do

  • "Your statement was sent to 42 customers on WhatsApp."
  • "We watched 11 technicians work before designing this screen."
  • "This works offline. Photos upload when you have signal."
  • Admit limits and say what to do instead.

Don't

  • "Revolutionise your workflow with smart AI-powered solutions!"
  • "Oops! Something went wrong 😅"
  • "Leading", "first", "best" without proof.
  • Translate English copy line by line into Arabic.

Logo Concepts

The final mark was drawn by the founder. The concepts explored on the way are kept as a record. Symbols are shown on the same 24-unit grid.

Final · v1 · founder's drawing

1 · The H

Idea
A single, soft H. Two swelling stems, rounded at every end, joined by a crossbar that slopes gently down to the right. One shape, no parts, no outline.
Metaphor
Fabric turned and stitched at a hem: soft, finished and strong. The stems read like two folded panels; the crossbar is the seam that holds them. No loose threads.
Geometry
Drawn freehand as one closed path, then fitted to a 24-unit grid at 22 high (about 0.65 : 1). The left stem sits higher than the right, so the letter leans into a quiet forward motion. Never redrawn; every file is generated from the master.
Why it works
Most H marks are two posts and a bar. This one has weight, softness and movement, so it is recognisable as a shape before it is read as a letter, which is what an app icon needs.
Strengths
Bold at 16px, calm at billboard size, one colour everywhere. The name is set beside it in Readex Pro, whose round, open shapes match the stems.
Watch for
Its softness depends on the curves; it must never be traced into straight lines, outlined, or set in a font "H" as a shortcut.

Lockup family

All lockups are outlined vectors, so no fonts are needed. The Arabic and Kurdish names are shaped with HarfBuzz from Vazirmatn (weight 600) and merged into single paths. In right-to-left lockups the H sits on the right, at the start of the line, and is never mirrored.

Stacked bilingual · signage, avatars, cover pages
Bilingual horizontal · documents, website header
Arabic · Kurdish Sorani

Construction

Proportions

One closed shape, about 0.65 wide to 1 high. The left stem starts higher and the right stem ends lower, so the H leans gently forward; the crossbar between them slopes down to the right at about 21°. Every end is rounded, and the stems swell slightly towards their ends, which is what makes the mark feel soft rather than drawn with a ruler. In the horizontal lockup the H is 1.4 × the cap height of the name, centred on the capitals, with a gap of 0.44 × the cap height.

Clear space

Half the width of the H on all sides, for the mark and for every lockup.

Minimum size

Mark: 16px on screen (it has no fine detail, so there is no separate small cut). Horizontal lockup: 88px wide on screen, 22 mm in print. Mark in print: 5 mm high.

Wordmark

"Hemlo" in title case, set in Poppins SemiBold (600) at −1% tracking and outlined, so no font is needed. Poppins is geometric and round, so it sits calmly beside the soft H without competing with it. Branch lockups put the branch name under "Hemlo" in Poppins Light (300) at 0.58 × the cap height, and the H grows to span both lines. Headlines stay in Readex Pro; Poppins is reserved for the logo

Incorrect usage

  • Redrawing, tracing, straightening or "cleaning up" the H; always use the master files.
  • Rotating, mirroring or slanting it, including in right-to-left layouts.
  • Replacing it with a letter H from a font.
  • Outlines, strokes, split colours or gradients inside the H; it is always one solid colour.
  • Adding a ring, a border or stamp-style text around it (the company stamp is the one exception).
  • Shadows, 3D or bevels on screen (emboss, foil and brass are for physical objects only).
  • Placing it on busy photos without a solid plate.
  • Brass as the only colour at small sizes (too little contrast).
  • Setting the name in a different font or weight next to the H.

Color Exploration

Three directions, judged on distinctiveness, working range in a dashboard and associations in Iraq.

Chosen

A · Kohl & Brass

Deep blue-black ink, muted brass, cool stone neutrals. Brass references Baghdad's coppersmiths' market (Souq al-Safafeer): craft, tools and precision, not luxury. Ink carries the seriousness; brass appears sparingly. It belongs to no big software brand.

B · Graphite & Signal Cobalt

Near-black graphite with a saturated cobalt. Clean and safe, but it reads as every developer tool and sits close to the blues of Atlassian and Microsoft. Rejected for lacking a point of view.

C · Tigris Green & Sand

Deep river green with sand neutrals. Warm and local, but green reads as banking and fintech, overlaps with the flag and with Careem, and warm sand drifts toward the overused cream look. Rejected.

Why these faces

  • Readex Pro has editorial character without trendiness. It sets the name beside the H, so headlines and logo agree; its rounded, open letters echo the soft stems of the mark.
  • IBM Plex Sans + Mono suit the "engineered" personality and handle dense numbers well. Plex is used for Latin only.
  • Vazirmatn (display) has square, geometric forms that echo the mark. Vazirmatn (text) is highly legible and supports Sorani.
  • IBM Plex Sans Arabic was rejected: it has an open, unresolved rendering bug for Central Kurdish.
  • Noto Kufi Arabic was replaced by Vazirmatn: Kufi's square geometry fought the soft H, while Vazirmatn is rounded, modern and supports every Sorani letter (ێ ۆ ڕ ڵ ڤ).

Arabic rhythm rules

  • Arabic sets one step larger than Latin at the same role, with line-height 1.7–1.8 (Latin 1.5).
  • Never letter-space Arabic; never use kashida to justify text in the UI.
  • Arabic punctuation: ، ؛ ؟ and « » quotes. Don't mix in Latin commas.
  • Digits: Western 0–9 in product UI and documents for IDs, phones and money. Eastern Arabic digits are optional in editorial Arabic, never mixed in one table.
  • Currency: 1,250,000 د.ع in Arabic, 1,250,000 IQD in English. No decimals.

Iconography

System

  • Base set: Lucide (open source, 1,500+ icons), so a solo founder never draws routine icons.
  • 24px grid, 1.75 stroke (Lucide's 2 is slightly heavy next to Plex), round caps and joins.
  • Outline by default; filled only for the active state in navigation.
  • Sizes: 16 (inline), 20 (buttons, tables), 24 (navigation), 32 (empty states).
  • Icon colour follows text colour. Never brass except the active product indicator.
  • Custom icons (QR tag, deposit, landmark address) are drawn on the same grid and stroke.

Right-to-left rules

  • Mirror anything that shows direction or sequence: arrows, back/forward, send, reply, progress, list indent, "next".
  • Don't mirror clocks, checkmarks, play buttons, search, cameras, phones, charts, or anything representing a real object.
  • Mirror with CSS ([dir=rtl] .icon-dir{transform:scaleX(-1)}) on a flagged subset, not globally.

Imagery

Documentary, not staged. The subject is the work and the tools people already use.

What we photograph

  • The workaround, Hemlo's signature subject: the debt notebook by the till, the stamped receipt, the WhatsApp group on a cracked phone, the Excel sheet printed and annotated.
  • Hands at work: a technician's hands on an outdoor AC unit, an accountant's desk with invoices, a broker's folder at a port office.
  • Real places: shop counters, workshops, warehouses, market lanes, rooftops, offices with plastic chairs and good light.
  • Product in context: the phone in a real hand in a real place, not floating.

How it looks

  • Natural light, slightly cool, true colour. No teal-orange grading, no heavy filters.
  • Eye-level or over-the-shoulder; people doing, not posing. Faces only with written consent.
  • Shot by Iraqi photographers in real businesses that agreed to take part. Credit them.
  • Never: handshakes, pointing at charts, laptop-and-latte offices, stock "Arab businessman", skyline composites.
  • Blur customer names, phone numbers and amounts on real documents.

Motion

Motion explains cause and effect. It never entertains.

Principles and timings

Micro (hover, press)
120ms, standard ease
Standard (toggle, menu)
180ms
Enter (panel, sheet)
240ms, 6px travel + fade
Exit
160ms, faster than enter
Easing
Standard cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1); exit cubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1). No bounce, no spring overshoot.

Behaviours

  • Signature "settle": the name settles, then the H slides into place beside it (hero, app launch, video end frames). Once per session, never looping.
  • Hover: colour and border change only; no lifting cards.
  • Loading: skeletons in stone for layout; a thin progress bar for known duration. No spinners over 1 second without text saying what's happening.
  • Saved/synced: a quiet inline confirmation ("Saved on this phone", then "Uploaded").
  • All motion is off under prefers-reduced-motion.

What Makes Hemlo Distinctive

A mark that is also a product system

The H is always Hemlo; in a product lockup it takes the product colour, so the logo shows who made it and which product it is. The logo explains the company structure without words.

A concept tied to real behaviour

"Earns its place" is a test the company runs on itself, not a slogan. It shapes the roadmap, the copy and the website's honest "Researching" labels.

Iraqi through details

Dinars without decimals, landmark addresses, notebooks in the photography, Sorani-ready type. No lions, no cuneiform.

A palette nobody in the category owns

Kohl ink and Safafeer brass: serious, warm, crafted, and far from tech blue, fintech green and startup purple.

Documentary proof instead of stock promises

The workaround photographed as it is. Research published as field notes.

Bilingual by design

Arabic copy is written, not translated, with its own tagline.

Things Hemlo Should Never Become

  • A software agency that builds anything for anyone
  • A one-size-fits-all ERP with a module for everything
  • A brand that claims "first", "leading" or "AI-powered" as its identity
  • A government-looking portal: seals, flags, all caps, dense forms
  • A gradient-and-glow startup page
  • A logo system where every product invents its own mark
  • A company whose Arabic is an afterthought
  • A brand that shows customers it doesn't have
  • Noisy: pop-ups, confetti, countdown timers
  • Cheap: discount banners and "limited offer" pressure

Hemlo is an Iraqi software company that builds small, focused products for the everyday work of businesses. It studies how work is really done (the notebooks, the WhatsApp groups, the stamped receipts) and builds software only for the parts that cost people time or money. Every product carries the same promise: software that earns its place.
