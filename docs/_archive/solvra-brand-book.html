Positioning statement

For Iraqi businesses whose daily work runs on WhatsApp, notebooks and spreadsheets, Solvra builds focused software products, each designed around one job we have studied inside real workplaces. Unlike general ERPs and imported tools, a Solvra product does its one job completely, fits how Iraqi businesses actually operate, and has to prove its value in daily use.

Manifesto

Most work in Iraq already has a system. It's a notebook, a WhatsApp group, a stamp and a person who remembers everything.

We don't pretend that system is broken. We study it, find the parts that cost people time or money, and build software only for those parts.

Small products. Real problems. Plain words. Software that works when the signal drops and the day gets busy.

If it doesn't earn its place, we don't ship it.

06

Brand Principles

Rules for product and design decisions. When two options compete, these break the tie.

1. Understand before building

No feature ships without a real example from a real workplace behind it.

2. One job, done completely

A narrow product that finishes the job beats a wide one that half-does ten.

3. Design for real conditions

Weak signal, a cracked phone, 48°C, cash, paper, Arabic. The hardest condition sets the standard.

4. Localise the details, not the decoration

IQD formatting, landmark addresses and WhatsApp delivery show you're Iraqi. Palm trees and lions don't.

5. Plain words, exact numbers

If a sentence needs a second read, rewrite it.

6. Quiet confidence

One strong idea per screen, one accent per page, no effects that exist only to impress.

07

Brand Story

Walk into almost any business in Iraq and you'll find a system that works better than outsiders expect. Orders arrive on WhatsApp. Debts live in a notebook by the till. A technician's day is planned in a phone call. Receipts are stamped, photographed and forwarded. Someone always remembers where everything is.

That system holds together until it doesn't: a payment nobody recorded, a customer who calls five times for an update, a report assembled by hand at midnight, a shipment held because one document was wrong. The cost is small each time and large over a year.

Solvra started from a simple decision: don't build software for Iraq from a distance. Sit in the shop, read the notebook, follow the paperwork, then build only the part that genuinely needs software, and make that part simple enough to use on a busy day with a weak signal.

We're early. We don't claim to have transformed anyone's business. What we promise is a method: every Solvra product starts with real work we've studied, does one job properly, and stays only if it keeps earning its place in people's days.

Naming principles

  • Name what the customer manages, not the industry. "Solvra Import" reads like an importing company; Solvra Shipments doesn't.
  • One word, in plain English, that customers already say.
  • Every product has a fixed Arabic descriptor, chosen for meaning rather than translated.
  • No invented product words, no "Pro/Plus/AI", no numbers in names.
  • Plans and editions are named by size (Starter, Team, Business), never by metals or gems.

Hierarchy and the exception rule

  • Level 1: Solvra (company, account, billing, support)
  • Level 2: Solvra + descriptor (products)
  • Level 3: plain feature names inside products ("Deposit schedule", "Visit report"), never branded
  • A product may earn an independent name only if it later serves a different audience that doesn't benefit from the parent, and it keeps the endorsement "by Solvra".
  • The earlier working name "Wasl" retires; the first product is Solvra Shipments.

Voice & Tone

A capable colleague who has visited your business, explaining something clearly on a busy day.

Voice principles

  • Specific over impressive. "Send a visit report in one tap", not "streamline reporting".
  • Short sentences, active verbs. One idea per sentence.
  • Customer's words. "Debt", "receipt", "technician", never "AR ledger" or "workforce entity".
  • Numbers exact. 1,250,000 IQD, not "over a million".
  • Technical depth on request. Plain for owners; precise and open for developers.
  • Punctuation: full stops, commas, colons for lists. No exclamation marks in product UI. No ALL CAPS sentences.

Arabic and English tone

  • English: calm, direct, lightly warm. Sentence case everywhere.
  • Arabic product UI: clear Modern Standard Arabic, short, no flowery openings ("نود أن نحيطكم علماً…" is banned).
  • Arabic marketing and social: MSA with Iraqi warmth where it helps (الشغل، هسه sparingly). Never full dialect in written UI.
  • To owners: money, time, control. To staff: less typing, fewer mistakes. To developers: architecture, trade-offs, what didn't work.
  • About products: describe the job, show the screen, state the limit. "Solvra Shipments doesn't file your customs declaration. Your broker still does."

Do

  • "Your statement was sent to 42 customers on WhatsApp."
  • "We watched 11 technicians work before designing this screen."
  • "This works offline. Photos upload when you have signal."
  • Admit limits and say what to do instead.

Don't

  • "Revolutionise your workflow with smart AI-powered solutions!"
  • "Oops! Something went wrong 😅"
  • "Leading", "first", "best" without proof.
  • Translate English copy line by line into Arabic.

Logo Concepts

Revised in v2 (round) and refined in v3 (one continuous seam with perfect semicircular turns). Every concept is drawn on the same 24-unit grid so they can be compared fairly.

Recommended · v3

1 · The Seal

Idea
A round mark made of two identical pieces. One continuous seam runs between them, straight runs joined by two perfect semicircles, so the S is found in the gap rather than drawn.
Metaphor
In Iraq, a business says "this is ours, this is correct" with a round stamp (الختم). The Seal is Solvra's version: a mark of work done properly, made of two pieces (the work and the software) that fit exactly.
Geometry
Circle of radius 12 on a 24 grid. The seam is 1.6 wide: runs at 6, 12 and 18, joined by semicircles of radius 3. The turn radius is exactly half the run spacing, so every inner band ends in a perfect round cap. Piece B is piece A rotated 180°.
Why it's new
Round S logos usually draw the letter. This one is a solid disc cut by a single rounded seam with straight runs. It reads engineered and calm, not flowing, and has no dots or halves like a yin-yang.
Strengths
Neat at any size. Fits the round avatar crops of WhatsApp, Instagram and LinkedIn perfectly. Keeps the product system (piece B takes the product's colour). Simple to reproduce.
Weaknesses
Circle-plus-S is a crowded family, so the seam proportions must never be simplified into a plain S curve.

Excellent / excellent.

Recommended Logo Direction: The Seal

One round mark, two identical pieces, one exact fit. The S is the space between them.

SolvraTwo-tone · brand moments
SolvraReversed
SolvraTwo-tone on ink

Lockup family

All lockups are outlined vectors, so no fonts are needed. The Arabic and Kurdish names are shaped with HarfBuzz from Noto Kufi Arabic (weight 600) and merged into single paths. In right-to-left lockups the Seal sits on the right, at the start of the line, and is never mirrored.

Stacked bilingual · signage, avatars, cover pages
Bilingual horizontal · documents, website header
Arabic · Kurdish Sorani

Construction

Proportions

A circle of radius 12 on a 24 grid. One continuous seam, 1.6 units wide: horizontal runs at 6, 12 and 18, joined by two semicircular turns of radius 3 that reach x 6 and x 18. Because the turn radius is half the run spacing, each inner band (4.4 thick) ends in a perfect semicircle, and the caps are 5.2 at their deepest. There are no corners anywhere in the mark, only straight lines and true circles.

Clear space

A quarter of the mark's diameter on all sides. In lockups the gap between the mark and the wordmark is 0.42 × the cap height, and the mark is 1.05 × the cap height.

Minimum size

Symbol: 24px on screen with the master seam (1.6). At 24px and below use the small cut (seam 2.2, same centreline). Absolute minimum 16px. Lockup: 88px wide on screen, 22 mm in print. Symbol in print: 6 mm.

Wordmark

Title case "Solvra", drawn from Schibsted Grotesk SemiBold (600) at −2% tracking and outlined. For the final drawing, soften the corners of the stroke ends (about 8% of the stem width) so the letters share the Seal's rounded turns, without becoming a "rounded font". Also shorten the r arm, open the l–v spacing by 4%, and keep tracking at −2%. Title case keeps it friendly and avoids the government feel of all caps.

Incorrect usage

  • Rotating by 90° (it becomes a different shape). 180° is allowed because the mark is symmetric.
  • Mirroring it in right-to-left layouts.
  • Smoothing the seam into a flowing S curve (it becomes a yin-yang or a generic S).
  • Changing the seam width, turn radius or proportions (use only the master 1.6 or small-cut 2.2 files).
  • Adding a ring, a border or stamp-style text around it.
  • Gradients, shadows, 3D, or two different product colours on one mark.
  • Placing it on busy photos without a solid plate.
  • Brass as the only colour at small sizes (too little contrast).
  • Rebuilding the wordmark in a different font.

Color Exploration

Three directions, judged on distinctiveness, working range in a dashboard and associations in Iraq.

Chosen

A · Kohl & Brass

Deep blue-black ink, muted brass, cool stone neutrals. Brass references Baghdad's coppersmiths' market (Souq al-Safafeer): craft, tools and precision, not luxury. Ink carries the seriousness; brass appears sparingly. It belongs to no big software brand.

B · Graphite & Signal Cobalt

Near-black graphite with a saturated cobalt. Clean and safe, but it reads as every developer tool and sits close to the blues of Atlassian and Microsoft. Rejected for lacking a point of view.

C · Tigris Green & Sand

Deep river green with sand neutrals. Warm and local, but green reads as banking and fintech, overlaps with the flag and with Careem, and warm sand drifts toward the overused cream look. Rejected.

Why these faces

  • Schibsted Grotesk has editorial character without trendiness. It is also the base of the wordmark, so headlines and logo agree.
  • IBM Plex Sans + Mono suit the "engineered" personality and handle dense numbers well. Plex is used for Latin only.
  • Noto Kufi Arabic (display) has square, geometric forms that echo the mark. Noto Sans Arabic (text) is highly legible and supports Sorani.
  • IBM Plex Sans Arabic was rejected: it has an open, unresolved rendering bug for Central Kurdish.

Arabic rhythm rules

  • Arabic sets one step larger than Latin at the same role, with line-height 1.7–1.8 (Latin 1.5).
  • Never letter-space Arabic; never use kashida to justify text in the UI.
  • Arabic punctuation: ، ؛ ؟ and « » quotes. Don't mix in Latin commas.
  • Digits: Western 0–9 in product UI and documents for IDs, phones and money. Eastern Arabic digits are optional in editorial Arabic, never mixed in one table.
  • Currency: 1,250,000 د.ع in Arabic, 1,250,000 IQD in English. No decimals.

Arabic wordmark treatment

Set in Noto Kufi Arabic Bold, then adjusted by hand: the سو connection tightened, the baseline of ر and ا aligned to the Latin baseline so bilingual lockups sit on one line, and the ف counter opened to match the seam width. The symbol always sits at the start of the line. In right-to-left layouts it moves to the right side without being mirrored.

Arabic taglines

  • حلولٌ بإتقان. Primary Arabic tagline (website, marketing, social, stationery).
  • كل حلّ في مكانه. Secondary line for editorial and product pages.
  • برمجيات تستحق مكانها في عملك اليومي. Formal (documents, proposals).
  • من الدفتر إلى النظام. Campaign line ("From the notebook to the system").

Arabic website behaviour

  • Arabic is the default for visitors in Iraq; English is one tap away and remembered.
  • The whole layout mirrors (navigation, icons with direction, progress); the logo, charts' time axis and phone numbers do not.
  • Arabic copy is written separately from English, with the same structure and different sentences.
  • Fonts switch by lang attribute, never by guessing from characters.

Kurdish without redesign

  • Noto Kufi and Noto Sans Arabic already cover Sorani letters (ڕ ڵ ۆ ێ ە ڤ).
  • Kurdish name: سۆلڤرا. Same lockup rules as Arabic.
  • Add a ckb locale file and a Kurdish copywriter; no visual changes.
  • Badini (Latin-script Kurdish) uses the Latin system as it is.

Iconography

System

  • Base set: Lucide (open source, 1,500+ icons), so a solo founder never draws routine icons.
  • 24px grid, 1.75 stroke (Lucide's 2 is slightly heavy next to Plex), round caps and joins.
  • Outline by default; filled only for the active state in navigation.
  • Sizes: 16 (inline), 20 (buttons, tables), 24 (navigation), 32 (empty states).
  • Icon colour follows text colour. Never brass except the active product indicator.
  • Custom icons (QR tag, deposit, landmark address) are drawn on the same grid and stroke.

Right-to-left rules

  • Mirror anything that shows direction or sequence: arrows, back/forward, send, reply, progress, list indent, "next".
  • Don't mirror clocks, checkmarks, play buttons, search, cameras, phones, charts, or anything representing a real object.
  • Mirror with CSS ([dir=rtl] .icon-dir{transform:scaleX(-1)}) on a flagged subset, not globally.

Imagery

Documentary, not staged. The subject is the work and the tools people already use.

What we photograph

  • The workaround, Solvra's signature subject: the debt notebook by the till, the stamped receipt, the WhatsApp group on a cracked phone, the Excel sheet printed and annotated.
  • Hands at work: a technician's hands on an outdoor AC unit, an accountant's desk with invoices, a broker's folder at a port office.
  • Real places: shop counters, workshops, warehouses, market lanes, rooftops, offices with plastic chairs and good light.
  • Product in context: the phone in a real hand in a real place, not floating.

How it looks

  • Natural light, slightly cool, true colour. No teal-orange grading, no heavy filters.
  • Eye-level or over-the-shoulder; people doing, not posing. Faces only with written consent.
  • Shot by Iraqi photographers in real businesses that agreed to take part. Credit them.
  • Never: handshakes, pointing at charts, laptop-and-latte offices, stock "Arab businessman", skyline composites.
  • Blur customer names, phone numbers and amounts on real documents.

Motion

Motion explains cause and effect. It never entertains.

Principles and timings

Micro (hover, press)
120ms, standard ease
Standard (toggle, menu)
180ms
Enter (panel, sheet)
240ms, 6px travel + fade
Exit
160ms, faster than enter
Easing
Standard cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1); exit cubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1). No bounce, no spring overshoot.

Behaviours

  • Signature "fit": the two pieces of the mark slide together and lock (hero, app launch, video end frames). Once per session, never looping.
  • Hover: colour and border change only; no lifting cards.
  • Loading: skeletons in stone for layout; a thin progress bar for known duration. No spinners over 1 second without text saying what's happening.
  • Saved/synced: a quiet inline confirmation ("Saved on this phone", then "Uploaded").
  • All motion is off under prefers-reduced-motion.

What Makes Solvra Distinctive

A mark that is also a product system

Piece A is always Solvra; piece B belongs to the product. The logo explains the company structure without words.

A concept tied to real behaviour

"Earns its place" is a test the company runs on itself, not a slogan. It shapes the roadmap, the copy and the website's honest "Researching" labels.

Iraqi through details

Dinars without decimals, landmark addresses, notebooks in the photography, Sorani-ready type. No lions, no cuneiform.

A palette nobody in the category owns

Documentary proof instead of stock promises

The workaround photographed as it is. Research published as field notes.

Arabic copy is written, not translated, with its own tagline.

Solvra is an Iraqi software company that builds small, focused products for the everyday work of businesses. It studies how work is really done (the notebooks, the WhatsApp groups, the stamped receipts) and builds software only for the parts that cost people time or money. Its identity is calm and exact: an ink-and-brass palette, a mark made of two pieces that fit, and a voice that speaks plainly in Arabic and English. Every product carries the same promise: software that earns its place.
